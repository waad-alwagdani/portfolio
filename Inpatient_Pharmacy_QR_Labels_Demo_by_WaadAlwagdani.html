▶ Why it looks like this

One tag shape, four names, four colors

High alert, hazardous, LASA and cytotoxic now all render as the same small rectangular tag — only the label text and the color change — stacked in a single column so a second or third alert makes the column taller instead of crowding the row.

Name, background, tags — all yours

The generic name's color, the label's own background, and each tag's color are independent pickers, so the whole thing can match whatever your department settles on rather than a fixed beige card.

One window, sized to the date-gun tag

The recurring failure in the phenobarbital sticker you first shared was that the expiry year was printed as part of the permanent design, so a new batch with a different expiry broke the whole label. The expiry here is a dashed, empty window sized to the date-gun label your team already uses.

Strip or square, your choice

"Bin strip" keeps the wide, short shape that survives being grabbed on a pull-out drawer edge. "Square card" stacks the same information vertically for a shelf or box label where a wide strip doesn't make sense.

Tall man lettering — only for confirmed pairs

Applied selectively to non-proprietary names on a recognised look-alike/sound-alike list. The pairs below are drawn from the official ISMP list and are the ones most likely to matter in a controlled-substance safe — including phenobarbital itself.

One drug, one strength, one bin

ASHP's 2023 diversion-prevention guideline recommends that access to controlled substances be restricted to one drug and one strength at a time — print a separate unit for each strength you stock.

If "Print sheet" does nothing

The button opens the sheet in its own browser tab and prints from there, instead of asking the page embedded in this chat to print itself. If your browser still blocks the new tab, allow pop-ups for this page and press Print again, or open this label page directly in your browser and use its print command.
